# Daily Scrape — August 6, 2026

## Top Story
Prompt injection attacks are becoming solvable through layered defenses—Boris Cherny (Claude Code lead) reported on Threads that combining strong model alignment, prompt injection probes, and Auto Mode in Claude Code drops attack success rates to ~0%. This matters to SMB owners and consultants selling AI services because it directly addresses the security concern that's been blocking enterprise adoption of autonomous agents; if you can credibly claim near-zero vulnerability to prompt injection, you've removed a major sales objection.

## What People Are Saying
- The programming community is fractured on LLM usage in hobby projects—the Hacker News thread "Born Against, or why hobby programming communities are against LLM usage" (312 points, 343 comments) signals deep skepticism among builders about using AI tools for learning and craft, which contradicts the "AI for everyone" narrative most integration tool vendors are pushing.
- Greg Isenberg's YouTube video "These AI Marketing Agents Get You Customers" and Dan Martell's "These are the AI tools you need to run your business" suggest the market is shifting toward *agentic* AI (autonomous tools that take action) rather than chat-based assistants—this is where real value is being claimed for SMB operations.
- Microsoft's AI revenue is almost entirely dependent on OpenAI (Bloomberg disclosure via Hacker News, 68 points)—meaning if you're evaluating "Microsoft AI" as a differentiator, you're really just reselling OpenAI under a different brand, which matters for positioning and competitive advantage.

## Updates
- Claude Opus 5 shipped with improved prompt injection defenses documented in their system card (Boris Cherny, Threads)—Auto Mode now comes with built-in safeguards that material reduce attack surface.
- HyperProbe (YC S26) launched as an agents-for-production-debugging tool (Hacker News, 61 points)—this is the emerging category: AI agents that do specific, constrained work in live systems, not general chatbots.
- OpenAI customer service crisis: users reporting prepaid credits vanishing with no audit trail (Hacker News, 50 points)—this is a trust and transparency issue that affects any consultant recommending OpenAI-based solutions to clients.
